What Gauge Screen Mesh Should I Use?

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

"What gauge should I use?" is a great question, and the honest answer is that the weave matters more than people realize. The mesh you choose decides which bugs get in, how much you see through it, and how much breeze you feel. Here's how I match mesh to the home.

Standard 18x14 fiberglass: the default

The most common patio mesh is an 18-by-14 weave — 18 strands one way, 14 the other, per inch. It blocks mosquitoes and everyday insects, has great visibility and airflow, and it's the most affordable. For a lot of patios away from water, it's all you need.

Its weakness is the small stuff. Those tiny biting no-see-ums slip right through an 18x14 weave, which is the number one complaint I hear from folks near canals and the coast.

No-see-um mesh: the South Florida upgrade

No-see-um mesh uses a much tighter weave (often 20x20 or finer) to stop the tiny biting gnats a standard screen lets through. If you live near water — which in South Florida is most of us — this is the single best upgrade you can make. You give up a touch of airflow and the view gets slightly denser, but you actually get to sit outside at dusk.

Pet-resistant mesh: heavy and tough

This isn't about weave so much as strength. Pet-resistant screen is a thick vinyl-coated polyester, roughly seven times stronger than standard fiberglass, built to take claws and jumping pets without tearing. Most people use it only on the lower panels where pets reach, with standard or no-see-um above.

Privacy and solar mesh: denser on purpose

Privacy and solar screens are woven dense enough to block the view in and cut more sun. Great on street-facing sides or where the afternoon sun bakes you — but you wouldn't do the whole enclosure in it, because you'd lose airflow and your own view out.

How I actually spec it

For most South Florida homes I recommend a mix: no-see-um where you sit and near water, standard fiberglass where airflow and view matter most, pet-resistant on the lower panels if you have animals, and privacy mesh only on the sides that need it. One mesh for the whole enclosure is rarely the smartest choice.
