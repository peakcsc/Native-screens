How Long Does a Pool Screen Enclosure Last in Florida?

A well-built pool enclosure is a long-term investment — but the frame and the screen age very differently. Here's how long each part lasts in South Florida's sun, salt, and storms, and what you can do to extend it.

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

Frame vs screen: two different lifespans

  • The aluminum frame can last 20–30+ years, often the life of the home, if it's quality aluminum and maintained. This is why you rarely need a whole new cage.
  • The screen mesh typically lasts 5–7 years under South Florida sun before UV makes it chalky and brittle. This is the part that gets rescreened.

That gap is the key takeaway: your frame outlives your screen several times over, so most "my enclosure is failing" problems are really just a rescreen.

What shortens screen life

  • Intense, direct UV exposure (south- and west-facing panels fade first)
  • Salt air near the coast and canals
  • Storm debris and falling branches
  • Pets clawing lower panels
  • Cheap, thin mesh installed to cut corners

How to make it last longer

  • Rinse the screen and frame a few times a year to clear salt and grime
  • Trim branches that overhang the cage
  • Fix a single torn panel early before it spreads
  • Choose quality mesh (and pet-resistant screen on lower panels if you have pets)

Frequently asked questions

How long does pool cage screen last?

In South Florida, screen mesh typically lasts about 5–7 years before UV makes it chalky and brittle. The aluminum frame lasts far longer — often 20–30+ years.

Do I need a new enclosure when the screen fails?

Almost never. The frame usually outlives the screen several times over, so a rescreen restores the enclosure for a fraction of a rebuild.

How can I make my screen last longer?

Rinse off salt and grime a few times a year, trim overhanging branches, fix torn panels early, and use quality mesh.
