Patio Screen Frame Options: Aluminum vs Wood vs Vinyl

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

The frame is the bones of your screened patio — it's what holds everything up and keeps it standing through a storm. People ask me about wood and vinyl, and I get why, but in South Florida the answer is pretty clear once you've watched these materials age in our climate. Here's the honest comparison.

Aluminum: what we build with, and why

Modern South Florida enclosures are almost always aluminum, and it's not a coincidence. Aluminum:

  • Won't rot, warp, or feed termites.
  • Is light but strong, so it spans well and meets wind-load code.
  • Shrugs off sun and (with quality material) handles salt air.
  • Comes in bronze, white, and matte black to match your home.

It can last 20 to 30 years or more. For our heat, humidity, bugs, and hurricanes, it's simply the right tool.

Wood: beautiful, wrong climate

Wood framing looks great in a magazine, but South Florida is brutal on it. Our humidity and rain cause rot, our termites treat it like a buffet, and it needs constant sealing and painting to survive. I've been called out to more than one sagging, soft wood-framed porch where the owner spent years fighting it. Unless you want a maintenance project, I steer people away from wood for screened structures down here.

Vinyl: fine for some things, not the frame

Vinyl shows up in railings and some enclosure accents, and it resists rot. But as the primary structural frame for a screened patio in our wind zone, it doesn't match aluminum for strength and code performance, and it can get brittle under relentless UV over time. You'll see vinyl in trim and certain window systems, not holding up a code-rated screen cage.

What about the screen itself?

Don't confuse the frame with the mesh. The frame is aluminum; the screen inside it is usually fiberglass. So when someone debates "aluminum vs vinyl vs wood," the real South Florida answer for the structure is aluminum — every time.

My honest take

If a contractor is pitching you a wood or all-vinyl structural frame for a screened patio in this climate, ask hard questions about rot, termites, and code. For nine out of ten homes here, a quality aluminum frame with fiberglass screen is the build that lasts and passes inspection.
