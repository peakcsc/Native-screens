Best Screen Material for Patios: Fiberglass vs Aluminum

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

People use the word "screen" like it's one thing, but it isn't. The mesh you put in your patio changes how long it lasts, how well it keeps bugs out, and how much you'll see through it. After five decades of installing both, here's how I steer homeowners between the two most common choices: fiberglass and aluminum.

Fiberglass screen: the everyday workhorse

Fiberglass mesh is what we install on the large majority of South Florida patios, and for good reason. It's a coated glass fiber that flexes instead of creasing, so it doesn't dent or "memory-crease" the way metal can. It's forgiving, affordable, and easy to replace.

  • Pros: low cost, doesn't crease or dent, good visibility, easy to rescreen, comes in no-see-um and pet-resistant versions.
  • Cons: not as strong as metal against sharp impacts, and cheaper grades can sun-rot faster.

For the vast majority of patios and pool cages, this is what I recommend. It's the right balance of price and performance for our climate.

Aluminum screen: strong, but with tradeoffs

Aluminum wire mesh is tougher against physical damage and won't sag as easily on big spans. You'll sometimes see it on older enclosures. But it has real downsides in South Florida:

  • It creases permanently — once it's dented, that dent stays.
  • Near the coast, cheaper aluminum mesh can oxidize and corrode in our salt air.
  • It cuts visibility a little more than fiberglass and costs more.

Honestly, I rarely spec aluminum mesh for a standard patio anymore. The places it still makes sense are specialty situations — a high-traffic doorway panel that takes abuse, for instance.

Don't forget the frame

Here's where people get confused: the frame of a modern South Florida enclosure is almost always aluminum — that's the structure. The screen inside it is usually fiberglass. So when someone asks "aluminum or fiberglass," the real answer for most homes is "an aluminum frame with fiberglass mesh." You get the strength where you need it and the forgiving screen where you touch it.

My honest recommendation

For nine out of ten patios down here, I'd put in a quality aluminum frame with fiberglass screen — and upgrade to no-see-um mesh if you're anywhere near water, which in South Florida is most of us. If you have dogs or kids who lean on the lower panels, add pet-resistant mesh just on the bottom couple of feet. That combination gives you the best life for the money.
