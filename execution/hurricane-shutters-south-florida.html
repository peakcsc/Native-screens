Shutter styles we install

  • Accordion shutters. Permanently mounted beside the opening and pulled closed along a track. Fast to deploy and a popular all-around choice.
  • Roll-down shutters. Mount above the opening and roll down with a crank or motor. The easiest to close, great for large or hard-to-reach windows.
  • Bahama shutters. Hinged at the top and propped out over the window for shade year-round, then closed and locked for a storm.
  • Colonial shutters. Side-hinged panels that add curb appeal and close over the window when weather turns.
  • Storm panels. Removable aluminum or clear polycarbonate panels, the most affordable protection, stored away between storms.

Why homeowners add shutters

  • Storm protection. Approved shutters shield glass from wind-borne debris during hurricane season.
  • Possible insurance savings. Many Florida policies give a wind-mitigation credit for approved shutters. Check with your insurer.
  • Security and privacy. Closed shutters make it harder to see in or break in.
  • Shade and sun control. Bahama and colonial styles cut heat and glare even on clear days.

Common questions

What kind of hurricane shutters do you install?

Accordion, roll-down, Bahama, colonial and removable storm panels. Larry walks the house with you and recommends what fits each opening and your budget.

Which hurricane shutters are best?

It depends on the opening and how you want to use it. Accordion shutters are a popular all-around choice, roll-downs are the easiest to close, and Bahama and colonial shutters add shade and curb appeal while still protecting the window. Larry helps you pick at the free estimate.

Do hurricane shutters need a permit?

Yes. Hurricane protection products are permitted in Florida and must carry a Miami-Dade or Florida product approval. As a licensed contractor, we pull the permit and install to code.

Can hurricane shutters lower my insurance?

Many Florida homeowners see a wind-mitigation credit on their policy after adding approved shutters. Check with your insurer, since the exact credit depends on your policy and the product installed.
