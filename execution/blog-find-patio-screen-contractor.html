How to Find a Reliable Patio Screen Contractor Near Me

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

I'm going to give you the advice I'd give my own family if they were hiring someone to screen a patio — including the stuff that costs me business to tell you. There are a lot of good screen companies in South Florida and a fair number of bad ones, and the difference isn't always obvious until the job goes sideways.

Start with licensed and insured — non-negotiable

Ask for the license and proof of insurance before anything else. A licensed contractor can pull permits and is accountable to the state. If someone can't or won't show you, walk away. The cheapest quote in the stack is almost always the unlicensed one, and that discount disappears the moment something goes wrong on your property.

Look at how long they've actually been around

Screen companies come and go, especially after a busy storm season when everyone with a van suddenly does enclosures. A company that's been in your area for years — and will still be there when you need a warranty honored — is worth more than a rock-bottom price from someone you'll never find again.

Read the reviews, but read them right

Don't just look at the star rating. Read how the company responds to problems. Everybody has a rough job now and then; what matters is whether they made it right. I'd trust a company with a few handled complaints over one with nothing but vague five-stars.

Get a real, in-person estimate

A good contractor comes out, measures, looks at your structure, and talks through options. Be skeptical of a firm price over the phone — either it's padded or the "extras" are coming. The estimate should spell out mesh type, frame color, whether the permit is included, and the warranty.

Questions I'd ask every contractor

  • Are you licensed and insured, and will you pull the permit?
  • Do you do the work yourselves or subcontract it out?
  • What mesh and frame are you quoting, and why?
  • What's the warranty on labor and materials?
  • Can I see photos of recent local jobs?

The answers tell you a lot. When you deal with a family-run shop, you usually get the owner and the same crew — not a salesperson handing you off to a subcontractor you never meet.

My honest opinion

Price matters, but it should be the third or fourth thing you weigh, not the first. The right contractor saves you money over the life of the enclosure by building it once, correctly. The wrong one costs you twice.
