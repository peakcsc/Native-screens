How to Choose Screen Color to Match Your Home

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

Screen color seems like a small decision until you're staring at your patio every day — and then it matters a lot. The good news is there are really only a few choices, and once you understand what each one does, picking is easy. Here's how I guide homeowners.

Two colors you're really choosing

There are two color decisions: the frame and the mesh, and they do different jobs.

The frame is the structure you see — and it should match or complement your home's trim. The mesh is the screen itself, and its color mostly affects visibility and glare.

Frame color

Aluminum frames typically come in three go-to colors:

  • Bronze — the most popular in South Florida. Warm, neutral, hides dirt, and works with almost any home.
  • White — clean and bright; great with white trim and lighter homes, but shows grime a little more.
  • Matte black — modern and sharp, and it disappears against the view at a distance, which people love for a sleek look.

My simple rule: match the frame to your fascia, gutters, or window trim, and it'll look like it belongs there instead of bolted on.

Mesh color

This one surprises people: darker mesh actually lets you see out better. Charcoal and black screen cut glare and almost vanish when you look through it, while lighter screen can look hazy and shows dirt. That's why charcoal is the standard — it gives the clearest view and the cleanest look from inside and out.

Matching frame and mesh

A few combinations that consistently look great:

  • Bronze frame with charcoal mesh — the classic, works everywhere.
  • Matte black frame with black mesh — sleek and modern, best for contemporary homes.
  • White frame with charcoal mesh — bright frame, clear view.

My honest take

Don't overthink the mesh — go charcoal or black for the clearest view and skip the lighter screen that looks dingy in a year. Spend your attention on the frame color and match it to your home's existing trim. That one choice is what makes an enclosure look custom instead of generic.
