Pool Cage Maintenance: How to Make Your Screen Enclosure Last

A little upkeep goes a long way on a South Florida pool enclosure. These simple maintenance habits keep your screen clear, your frame sound, and delay the day you need a full rescreen.

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

A few times a year

  • Rinse the screen and frame. A hose (or a soft brush with mild soap) clears salt, pollen, and grime that degrade the mesh and dull the aluminum.
  • Clear the super gutters and downspouts. Leaves and debris cause overflow and backup in our heavy rain.
  • Check the spline. The rubber spline that holds the screen in the frame dries and lifts in the heat — press it back or have it replaced before panels billow.

After every storm

  • Walk the enclosure and look for torn panels, bent beams, or lifted anchors.
  • Fix a single torn panel early — it spreads fast on the next windy day.
  • Photograph any damage before repairs in case you file an insurance claim.

Keep trees in check

The most common cause of enclosure damage in South Florida isn't wind alone — it's branches falling on the cage. Trim anything overhanging the structure before hurricane season.

When to call a pro

Bent frame members, corroded uprights, failing anchors, or more than a couple of torn panels are worth a professional look. We offer free inspections and honest quotes — we'll tell you if it just needs a small fix.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I clean my pool cage?

Rinse the screen and frame a few times a year, and clear the gutters seasonally. More often if you're near the coast, canals, or heavy tree cover.

What's the most common cause of enclosure damage?

Falling tree branches, not wind alone. Trimming anything that overhangs the cage prevents most storm damage.

Should I fix one small tear right away?

Yes — a single torn panel spreads quickly in wind. Fixing one panel early is cheap; waiting can turn it into a full rescreen.
