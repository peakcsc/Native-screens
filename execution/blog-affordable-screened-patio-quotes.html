Screened Patio Budget: How to Get Affordable Quotes

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

Everybody wants a good price — I get it, and I'd want the same. But "affordable" and "cheapest" aren't the same thing, and chasing the lowest number is how people end up paying twice. Here's how to actually save money on a screened patio without getting burned, including how to compare quotes the right way.

Where you can genuinely save

  • Use your existing structure. Screening in a covered patio you already have is far cheaper than building a new roof and frame. Work with what's there.
  • Keep the roofline simple. A flat or mansard build costs less than a dome or elaborate shape, and you'll live in it the same.
  • Be selective with premium mesh. Put no-see-um or privacy screen only on the panels that need it, and standard fiberglass elsewhere.
  • Consider partial screening. If a wall of your house can serve as one side, you're not paying to screen it.
  • Rescreen instead of rebuild when the frame is still good — this is the single biggest saver.

Where saving money backfires

I've watched these go wrong for decades. The false economies:

  • Hiring unlicensed to save a few hundred dollars — then eating the cost when it fails inspection or a claim gets denied.
  • Thin, cheap mesh that sun-rots in a couple of years instead of five to seven.
  • Skipping the permit — which comes back at resale or after a storm.
  • Undersized framing that sags or doesn't survive a hurricane.

How to compare quotes the smart way

Get two or three quotes, but don't just compare the bottom-line price — compare what's in them. Make sure each spells out the same things: mesh type, frame color, whether the permit is included, and the warranty. A quote that's $800 cheaper often has thinner screen, no permit, or no warranty. Line them up apples to apples and the "cheap" one frequently isn't.

And to gather quotes fast: have your measurements and a couple of photos ready, tell each contractor exactly what you want, and ask for the estimate in writing with those four details. You'll get faster, more comparable numbers.

My honest take

The most affordable patio over its life is the one built once, correctly, with quality mesh and a permit — not the one with the lowest sticker that you redo in three years. Save money on scope and smart choices, not on the things that make it last.
