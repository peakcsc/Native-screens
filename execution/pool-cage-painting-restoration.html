Pool Cage Painting & Restoration in South Florida

If your pool cage frame is chalky, faded, or streaked with rust, you may not need a new enclosure — you may need a restoration. Repainting and restoring the aluminum frame makes a tired cage look new for a fraction of a rebuild.

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

What restoration includes

  • Frame cleaning — removing oxidation, mildew, and salt buildup.
  • Rust treatment on fasteners and any steel hardware.
  • Repainting the aluminum in a fresh, uniform color (bronze, white, or matte black).
  • Hardware and spline replacement as needed.
  • A rescreen to finish the refresh if the mesh is also aged.

Restore or replace?

If your frame is structurally sound but just looks old, restoration is by far the better value — the aluminum can last decades, so there's no reason to tear down a solid cage. We only recommend replacement when uprights are corroded through or the structure is compromised.

Great before selling

A restored, freshly painted and rescreened enclosure is one of the highest-return curb-appeal upgrades before listing a South Florida home — buyers notice a clean cage immediately.

Common questions

Can you paint an aluminum pool cage?

Yes — we clean, treat, and repaint the aluminum frame in a uniform color, which makes an old, chalky cage look new without replacing it.

Is it worth restoring an old pool cage?

If the frame is structurally sound, yes — restoration costs a fraction of a rebuild and the aluminum can last decades. We only recommend replacement if the structure is compromised.

Can you restore and rescreen at the same time?

Yes — combining a frame restoration with a fresh rescreen gives you a like-new enclosure in one visit.
