7 Signs Your Pool Cage Needs a Rescreen

Not sure if your pool enclosure needs new screen? In most cases you don't need a whole new cage — just a rescreen, which reuses your existing aluminum frame. Here are the 7 clearest signs it's time, from a South Florida company that's been rescreening since 1972.

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

How to tell if you need a rescreen

  1. Torn or ripped panels. Storms, falling branches, and pets open up holes that only grow. One torn panel is a quick fix; several means it's time.
  2. Sagging, loose screen. If the mesh is baggy or pulling out of the frame's spline channel, the screen has stretched past its life.
  3. Chalky, faded, grey mesh. Years of South Florida UV "sun-rot" the screen — it turns chalky and brittle and tears at a touch.
  4. Bugs getting through. If no-see-ums and mosquitoes are suddenly inside even though the frame looks fine, the mesh weave has failed.
  5. Visible daylight gaps. Light showing through seams or around the spline means the screen no longer seals.
  6. Spline popping out. The rubber spline that holds the screen in the frame dries out and lifts in our heat, letting panels billow.
  7. It just looks tired. Before selling a home or hosting, a fresh rescreen makes the whole enclosure look new for a fraction of a rebuild.

Rescreen vs. full rebuild

Here's the good news: the frame almost always outlasts the screen. If your aluminum structure is solid, a rescreen restores the enclosure for far less than a new cage — you're only paying for new mesh, not a new structure. We only recommend frame work if uprights are corroded or bent.

What a rescreen costs

In South Florida, a single torn panel runs about $35–$250, and a full pool cage rescreen typically runs $1,200–$4,500 depending on size and mesh. See our pool cage rescreen cost guide for the full breakdown.

Frequently asked questions

Can I just replace one torn panel?

Yes — if only a panel or two failed, we replace just those, usually $35–$250 each. You don't need a full rescreen.

How long does a rescreen take?

Most are done in a single day. Larger or storm-damaged enclosures can take longer.

Do you reuse my frame?

Yes — rescreening reuses your existing aluminum frame, so you only pay for new screen.
