Do You Need a Permit for a Screen Enclosure in Broward County?

Short answer: yes. New pool enclosures, screen rooms, and patio enclosures in Broward County and across South Florida require a building permit and must meet Florida wind-load code. Here's how permitting actually works — and why a licensed contractor handling it matters.

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

Why a permit is required

A screen enclosure is a permanent structure attached to your home, so Broward County and its cities require a building permit to make sure it meets the Florida Building Code and the area's wind-load requirements (South Florida is a High-Velocity Hurricane Zone in parts). The permit protects you: it means the enclosure is engineered and inspected to survive storm season.

Who pulls the permit?

A licensed contractor pulls the permit for you. That's a big reason to hire a licensed company rather than an unlicensed "handyman" price — the license is what lets them permit the job, and an unpermitted enclosure can cause problems when you sell your home or file an insurance claim.

What the permit process involves

  • Engineered drawings showing the enclosure meets wind-load code
  • Submittal to your city/county building department
  • Inspection(s) during and after the build
  • Final sign-off that closes the permit

Do rescreens need a permit?

Usually no. A rescreen (replacing the screen on an existing frame) typically does not require a permit because you're not changing the structure. A new enclosure or a structural rebuild does. We'll tell you exactly what your project needs during a free estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Does a pool enclosure need a permit in Broward County?

Yes — new pool and patio enclosures require a building permit and must meet Florida wind-load code. A licensed contractor pulls it for you.

Does rescreening need a permit?

Usually not. Replacing screen on an existing frame doesn't change the structure, so it typically needs no permit. New builds and structural rebuilds do.

What happens if my enclosure wasn't permitted?

An unpermitted enclosure can create issues when selling your home or filing an insurance claim. A licensed contractor can advise on permitting an existing structure.
