What Are the Downsides of a Screened-In Porch?

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

You won't hear many screen companies lead with the downsides, but after 50-plus years I'd rather you go in with your eyes open than feel oversold later. A screened-in porch is one of the best upgrades you can make in South Florida — I believe that genuinely — but it isn't perfect, and here's the honest list.

You still deal with the weather

Screen keeps bugs and leaves out, not rain and wind. In a hard South Florida downpour with wind behind it, you'll get some spray coming through the mesh. That's normal and by design — the screen is meant to let air and some water pass so the structure survives storms. If you want to stay bone-dry in any weather, that's a glass or solid enclosure, not a screen.

Screen needs upkeep

Mesh isn't forever. Our sun breaks it down over about 5 to 7 years, and you'll eventually need a rescreen. Branches, pets, and storms can tear a panel sooner. It's not expensive to maintain, but it's not zero, either.

It's not a fully private, sealed room

Standard screen lets people see in during the day to some degree, and it doesn't block noise or give you climate control. You can add privacy mesh on the panels that need it, but a screened porch is an open-air room by nature. If you want a sealed, air-conditioned space, that's a different project.

Permitting and code take time

A new porch enclosure has to be engineered and permitted to Florida wind-load code. That's a good thing for storm safety, but it does add time and cost compared to someone just slapping up screen with no paperwork. I'd never cut that corner, but it's fair to know it's part of the deal.

Cheap builds age badly

The biggest "downside" I see isn't screened porches — it's poorly built ones. Thin mesh, undersized framing, skipped permits. They sag, leak at the seams, and don't hold up in a storm. Built right, a screened porch is low-drama for decades. Built cheap, it's a recurring headache.

My honest take

For the way we live down here — bugs, sun, afternoon storms — the upsides of a screened porch crush the downsides for most families. Just know what it is: an open-air, bug-free room that needs an occasional rescreen, not a sealed sunroom. Go in with that expectation and you'll love it.
