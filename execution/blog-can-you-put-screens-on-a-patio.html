Can You Put Screens on a Patio? Your Options Explained

Quick answer: Yes. An existing covered patio can be screened into a bug-free room, and patio doors and windows can get screens too. The main options are a full screen enclosure, screening in an existing covered patio, and patio door or window screens.

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

Your main options

  • Screen in an existing covered patio. If you already have a roof or overhang, we frame and screen the open sides to create a usable, bug-free room.
  • Full patio screen enclosure. A custom aluminum-framed enclosure over an open patio or pool deck, built to Florida code.
  • Patio doors with screens. Sliding patio doors can get sliding screen doors; we repair and replace these too.
  • Patio windows with screens. Window screens keep the breeze and block the bugs.

What's needed

Screening an open patio into a new enclosure requires a permit and must meet Florida wind-load code — as a licensed contractor we handle the engineering and permit. Screening an existing covered patio is usually simpler. We'll walk the options on a free estimate.

Why screen your patio in South Florida

Between no-see-ums, afternoon storms, and harsh sun, an open patio sits empty most of the year here. Screens give you a shaded, breezy, bug-free room you'll actually use — one of the best value upgrades a South Florida home can make.

Frequently asked questions

Can you screen in an existing covered patio?

Yes — if you have a roof or overhang, we frame and screen the open sides to make a bug-free room. We assess the structure on a free estimate.

Do sliding patio doors come with screens?

Many do, but screens wear out or tear. We repair and replace sliding patio door screens, including rollers and torn mesh.

Do I need a permit to screen my patio?

A new screen enclosure needs a permit and must meet Florida code — we handle it. Screening an existing covered patio is usually simpler.
