How to Prepare Your Pool Enclosure for Hurricane Season in Florida

A screen enclosure is built to take South Florida weather, but hurricane-force wind is another level. Here's a practical checklist to protect your pool cage before a storm — and exactly what to do if it gets damaged.

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

Before the storm

  • Remove loose items from the enclosure. Furniture, planters, and screens left inside become projectiles that tear the mesh from the inside.
  • Trim overhanging branches. The #1 cause of enclosure damage in South Florida isn't wind alone — it's limbs falling onto the cage.
  • Check your spline and fasteners. Loose screen and worn hardware fail first. A quick pre-season inspection catches weak points.
  • Know your mesh is designed to release. Screen panels are meant to blow out before the aluminum frame fails — that's by design, and it protects the structure. A torn panel after a storm is normal and far cheaper than a bent frame.
  • Photograph your enclosure now. Date-stamped photos before the season help if you need to file an insurance claim later.

After the storm

  • Don't ignore small tears. A single ripped panel spreads fast in the next windy day. Fixing one panel is cheap; waiting turns it into a full rescreen.
  • Check the frame and super gutters. Look for bent beams, lifted anchors, and clogged or detached gutters.
  • Document damage for insurance. Storm damage to a pool enclosure is often covered — photograph everything before repairs.
  • Call early. After a storm, every screen company is slammed. Getting on the list early means faster repair.

Frequently asked questions

Is pool enclosure storm damage covered by insurance?

Often yes — screen enclosure damage from a named storm is frequently covered. Photograph the damage before repairs and check with your insurer.

Why did my screen blow out but my frame is fine?

That's by design. Screen panels are meant to release under extreme wind so the aluminum frame survives. A torn panel is a far cheaper fix than a bent structure.
