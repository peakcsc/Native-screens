Common screen door problems we fix

  • Sagging or dragging doors — worn hinges or a racked frame throw the door out of square.
  • Broken rollers or wheels on sliding screen doors (common on lanais and patios).
  • Torn screen in the door panel — a quick rescreen of just the door.
  • Broken latches, handles, and closers that won't keep the door shut (letting bugs in).
  • Kick plates — adding or replacing the lower panel that takes foot traffic and pet wear.

Repair or replace?

Often a door just needs new rollers, a hinge, or a rescreen — an inexpensive fix. If the door frame is bent or corroded, a new door panel is the better long-term call. We'll tell you honestly which makes sense.

Common questions

How much does it cost to fix a screen door?

A screen door rescreen is typically $85–$350; roller, hinge, and latch fixes are modest. We give free, exact quotes.

Can you fix a sliding screen door that won't roll?

Yes — usually it just needs new rollers or track cleaning. If the frame is bent, a new door is the better fix.

Do you add kick plates to screen doors?

Yes — a kick plate protects the lower panel from foot traffic and pets and extends the life of the door screen.

Can you fix just the screen door?

Yes — Native Screens repairs or replaces pool-cage and patio screen doors across South Florida, including rollers, hinges, latches, and torn panels, often the same day.
