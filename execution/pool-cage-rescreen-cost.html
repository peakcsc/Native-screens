What affects the cost of a rescreen?

  • Size & height of the enclosure. Bigger, taller, multi-story, or panoramic "picture window" cages take more material and labor.
  • Mesh type. Standard 18×14 fiberglass is the most affordable. No-see-um mesh (blocks tiny biting bugs), pet-resistant mesh, and privacy screen cost more.
  • Frame condition. If uprights are corroded, beams are bent, or super gutters are failing, those repairs add to the total.
  • How many panels. One torn panel is a quick fix; a full rescreen replaces every panel.

Which pool screen mesh should you choose?

  • Standard fiberglass (18×14): the most common and most affordable. Charcoal color disappears at a distance better than grey.
  • No-see-um mesh: a tighter weave that keeps out the tiny biting gnats a standard screen lets through — popular across South Florida.
  • Pet-resistant mesh: heavier, tear-resistant screen for homes with dogs and cats.

Do you need a whole new frame?

Usually not. Rescreening reuses your existing aluminum frame — you're only paying for new screen, not a brand-new enclosure. That's why a rescreen costs a fraction of a full rebuild. We'll only recommend frame work if the structure itself is damaged.

How long does a pool cage rescreen take?

Most rescreens are done in a single day. Larger or storm-damaged enclosures can take longer — you'll get an exact timeline with your free estimate.
