No-See-Um Screen vs Standard Screen: Which Is Best for South Florida?

If the tiny biting bugs still get through your screen, you probably have standard mesh — not no-see-um. Here's the honest comparison of the two most common screen types for South Florida pool cages and patios, so you can choose the right one.

Last updated: November 2026 · Native Screens, serving South Florida since 1972

The quick answer

Standard fiberglass screen (18x14) blocks mosquitoes and normal insects. No-see-um screen (20x20 or tighter) blocks the much smaller biting gnats — "no-see-ums" — that a standard screen lets right through. If you get bitten on your own lanai at dusk, no-see-um mesh is the fix.

Which should you choose?

  • Choose standard if you're not bothered by tiny bugs and want maximum airflow and the lowest cost.
  • Choose no-see-um if you're near water, canals, or mangroves (most of coastal South Florida), or if you're getting bitten despite having screens.
  • Pet owners: ask about pet-resistant mesh for the lower panels — tougher against claws.

Many homeowners do a hybrid: no-see-um on the sides where they sit, standard elsewhere. We'll recommend the right mix for your home during a free estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Does no-see-um screen reduce airflow a lot?

Only slightly. The weave is tighter, so airflow is modestly reduced, but most people don't notice a meaningful difference in comfort.

Is no-see-um screen worth the extra cost?

If you get bitten on your patio or live near water, yes — it blocks the tiny gnats a standard screen can't. If bugs aren't an issue, standard is fine.

Can you mix screen types on one enclosure?

Yes — a common approach is no-see-um where you sit and standard elsewhere to balance cost and comfort.
