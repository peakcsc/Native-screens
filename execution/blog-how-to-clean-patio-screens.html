How to Clean Patio Screens Without Damaging Them

Quick answer: Rinse patio screens with a garden hose, then gently wash with mild soap and a soft brush or microfiber cloth, and rinse again. Do not use a pressure washer — it tears the mesh and loosens the spline that holds it in the frame.

Step by step

  1. Dry brush first. Knock off loose dirt, cobwebs, and pollen with a soft brush or broom.
  2. Rinse the screen and frame with a regular garden hose (gentle flow).
  3. Wash with a bucket of warm water and a little mild dish soap, using a soft-bristle brush or microfiber cloth. Work top to bottom.
  4. Rinse again and let it air dry.
  5. Wipe the aluminum frame to clear salt and grime, which dull the finish over time.

What not to do

  • No pressure washers. They stretch and tear mesh and blow the spline out of the frame.
  • No harsh chemicals or bleach — they can degrade the screen and discolor the frame.
  • Don't scrub hard on brittle, sun-rotted screen — if it's chalky and tearing, it needs a rescreen, not cleaning.

How often

A rinse every month or two and a full wash a few times a year keeps South Florida salt, pollen, and grime from building up. If the mesh is grey, chalky, or tearing when you touch it, cleaning won't help — it's time for a rescreen.

Frequently asked questions

Can you pressure wash patio screens?

No — a pressure washer tears the mesh and loosens the spline. Use a garden hose, mild soap, and a soft brush instead.

What's the best cleaner for patio screens?

Warm water with a little mild dish soap is all you need. Avoid bleach and harsh chemicals, which degrade screen and frame.

How often should I clean my patio screens?

Rinse every month or two and do a full wash a few times a year — more often near the coast where salt builds up.
