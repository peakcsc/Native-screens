Bigger isn't always better with a screened patio, and I've talked plenty of homeowners out of a larger build than they needed. The right size is the one that matches how you'll actually live in the space — not the biggest footprint you can fit. Here's how I help people think it through.

Start with how you'll use it

Before we talk square footage, I ask one question: what's going to happen out here? The answer changes everything.

  • Morning coffee and a couple of chairs? A modest covered area is plenty.
  • Dining table and entertaining? You need real room to walk around furniture.
  • Screening a pool? Now the size is dictated by the deck and the pool, not preference.

Design around the furniture and the foot traffic, and you'll land on a size that feels right instead of either cramped or echoey.

The value sweet spot

In my experience, the best return comes from a patio that's generous enough to be genuinely usable — you can host, not just sit — without pushing into a huge custom span that drives up engineering and cost. A space that comfortably holds a dining set and a seating area tends to be the one families actually live in and that buyers notice.

Go too small and it becomes a glorified walkway nobody uses. Go too big and you pay for height, beams, and panels you rarely need, while the room feels empty.

Where cost jumps with size

Price doesn't rise in a straight line. A couple of things cause step-ups:

  • Wide spans need stronger beams and sometimes support posts, which cost more and can interrupt the view.
  • Taller enclosures mean more material and harder labor.
  • Panoramic "picture window" builds at larger sizes need heavier engineering.

Knowing where those jumps are lets you get 90% of the benefit without paying for the last expensive 10% you won't use.

My honest opinion on resale

A well-built, right-sized screened patio adds real appeal in South Florida because buyers here expect usable outdoor space. But an oversized, awkward enclosure doesn't add proportionally more value — sometimes it reads as "too much." Build for how you'll live, and the resale takes care of itself.
