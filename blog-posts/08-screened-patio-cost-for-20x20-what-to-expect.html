A 20x20 is one of the most common patio sizes we quote in South Florida — big enough to actually live in, small enough to stay reasonable. So let me give you a realistic picture of what a project that size tends to run and, more importantly, what makes it swing.

Ballpark for a 20x20

A 20x20 is 400 square feet, which puts it solidly in the mid-range of patio projects. Depending on the details, most 20x20 screen enclosures in our area land somewhere in the mid four figures to low five figures. Screening in an existing covered 20x20 is on the lower end; building a brand-new free-standing enclosure with its own roof structure is on the higher end.

I won't give you a single magic number, because anybody who quotes a 20x20 without seeing it is guessing. But that range will keep you from getting shocked either direction.

What pushes a 20x20 up or down

  • Do you already have a roof or cover? If yes, you're mostly paying for framing the sides and screening — much cheaper. If no, the roof structure is a big part of the cost.
  • Height. A standard-height enclosure costs less than a tall, open, cathedral-style one.
  • Mesh. Standard fiberglass keeps it affordable; no-see-um or privacy mesh adds some.
  • Roofline and style. A simple flat or mansard costs less than a dome or panoramic build.
  • Doors and extras. Extra screen doors, pet doors, and super gutters add up.

A quick story on why "20x20" alone isn't enough

We quoted two 20x20 jobs the same month that came in very differently. One was a screen-in of an existing covered lanai — straightforward, affordable. The other was an open paver patio with no cover, where the homeowner wanted a full aluminum roof and a panoramic screen wall facing the lake. Same footprint, very different builds, very different prices. The square footage was identical; everything else wasn't.

Getting the most value at this size

If you're working to a budget on a 20x20, the smartest moves are usually: keep the roofline simple, use quality standard mesh with a no-see-um upgrade only where you sit, and skip the extras you won't use. You'll get a patio that feels great without paying for flourishes that don't change how you live in it.
