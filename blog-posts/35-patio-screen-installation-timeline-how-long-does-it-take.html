Once people decide to screen their patio, the next question is always "how long will this take?" Fair — you want your backyard back. The honest answer ranges from a single day to a few weeks, and the biggest variable isn't the building at all. Here's a realistic timeline.

Rescreens: usually one day

If your frame is already up and you're just getting new screen, most rescreens are done in a single day. A small patio or a few panels can be a couple of hours; a large pool cage might take most of the day. It's the fastest, least disruptive work we do.

New enclosures: the build is quick, the permit isn't

Here's what surprises people: the actual construction of a new screen enclosure often takes just a few days to about a week once we start. Aluminum goes up fast. What stretches the timeline is everything before that:

  • The estimate and design — measuring, choosing mesh and style, finalizing the quote.
  • Engineering and permitting — this is the long pole. Getting plans approved by the county can take a few weeks depending on the jurisdiction and how busy they are.
  • Inspection — there's an inspection step to close the permit.

So from "yes, let's do it" to "it's finished," a new enclosure is often a few weeks, with the building itself being the short part.

What can slow things down

  • Permit backlogs, especially after a busy storm season when everyone's building at once.
  • Custom or large builds that need more engineering.
  • Weather — we don't install aluminum in a lightning storm.
  • Material lead times on special colors or panoramic systems.

How to keep it moving

  • Have your decisions made — mesh, color, layout — when we quote, so nothing stalls.
  • Hire licensed, so the permit actually gets pulled correctly the first time.
  • Book before peak season if you can; spring and post-storm are the busiest.

My honest take

Don't judge a contractor by who promises the fastest finish — judge them by who's honest about the permit timeline. If someone says they'll build your new enclosure in two days with no mention of permitting, that's a red flag, not a selling point. Done right, the wait is mostly the county, and it's worth it.
