Yes — and partial screening is a smart, budget-friendly option more homeowners should consider. You don't always have to enclose all four sides. Depending on your home's layout, screening three sides (or even fewer) can give you most of the benefit for less money. Here's when it makes sense.

When three-sided screening works great

The most common setup is a patio tucked against the house on one side. In that case, your home's wall is the fourth side — so you only need to screen the three open sides. This is extremely common and totally legitimate; the house closes the loop.

  • A patio set into an L-shaped house, using two existing walls.
  • A lanai under the roofline with the back wall of the home as one side.
  • A covered area where one side is already solid.

The benefits of going partial

  • Lower cost — fewer panels, less frame, less labor.
  • It still keeps bugs out, because the solid wall seals that side completely.
  • It can look cleaner, tying the enclosure into the house rather than wrapping an unnecessary screen wall against a solid wall.

What to watch out for

The key is that the unscreened side has to actually be sealed — a solid wall, properly joined to the enclosure. Where people get in trouble is leaving a gap between the screen frame and the house, which lets bugs right in. Done correctly, we tie the frame tight to the wall so there's no gap. Done sloppily, a "three-sided" enclosure leaks bugs at the seam.

When you want all four

If your patio sits out in the open, away from the house, or you want a freestanding pool cage, then you're screening all sides by necessity. And sometimes people screen a fourth side even against a wall for a finished, uniform look. That's a preference call, not a requirement.

My honest take

Don't pay to screen a side that a solid wall already handles. If your layout allows three-sided screening, it's a genuine way to save without giving up the bug protection — as long as the enclosure is sealed tight to the house. I'll point it out when your home is a good candidate.
