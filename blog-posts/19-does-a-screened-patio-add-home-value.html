I get asked this a lot, usually by homeowners trying to justify the project to a spouse. Here's my honest answer after decades of watching these go in across South Florida: yes, a well-built screened patio adds value here — but how you build it matters, and the biggest return isn't always the number on an appraisal.

Why it adds value in South Florida specifically

Down here, outdoor living isn't a bonus — buyers expect it. A screened patio or pool cage turns an unusable, bug-ridden, sun-baked yard into a room families actually use most of the year. That's exactly the lifestyle people are shopping for when they move to Florida, so it shows up as real appeal when you sell.

And a screened pool cage is close to mandatory in a lot of neighborhoods. A pool without one reads as "unfinished" and "about to be a mosquito farm" to a buyer.

The kind of return to expect

A screened patio generally returns a healthy share of its cost at resale and, just as important, helps the home sell faster because it checks a box buyers care about. It's not a guaranteed dollar-for-dollar payback like some renovations claim to be — nobody should promise you that — but it's one of the better-received outdoor upgrades for the money in our market.

What makes it add value vs. hurt

  • Adds value: clean, code-built, permitted, right-sized for the home, well-maintained screen.
  • Hurts value: unpermitted work (shows up in inspection and scares buyers), sagging old mesh, a cheap build that looks tired, or an oddly oversized enclosure that overwhelms the yard.

I've watched unpermitted enclosures delay or sink sales. Doing it right isn't just about storm safety — it protects the value you're trying to add.

My honest take

Build a screened patio because you'll love living in it — that's the real return, and you collect it every single day, not just at closing. The resale bump is a nice bonus on top. Just build it permitted, right-sized, and well, so it's an asset when you sell instead of a question mark.
