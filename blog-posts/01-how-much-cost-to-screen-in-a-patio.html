After more than 50 years of screening in patios across South Florida, the first question we get on almost every call is the same one: "What's this going to cost me?" I understand why — nobody wants a surprise. So let me give you real numbers, not a sales pitch, and explain what actually moves the price up or down.

The short answer

For most South Florida homes, screening in a patio or porch runs somewhere between $2,000 and $9,000. If you're building a full screen room or a large lanai from scratch, it can climb to $25,000 or more. And if you just have a torn panel or two, you might be looking at $35 to $250 — a world away from a full project.

That's a wide range on purpose. I've quoted a small covered porch at under $2,500 and a big panoramic pool-area enclosure at five figures in the same week. The difference is almost always size, height, and what you want out of it.

What actually drives the price

When my crew prices a job, we're really looking at a handful of things:

  • Size and height. This is the biggest factor by far. More square footage means more aluminum, more screen, and more labor. Tall two-story cages cost more because of the equipment and safety involved.
  • Whether you already have a structure. Screening in an existing covered patio is far cheaper than building a brand-new enclosure, because the roof and posts are already there.
  • The mesh you choose. Standard fiberglass is the most affordable. No-see-um mesh, pet-resistant screen, and privacy screen all cost a bit more — but down here, the no-see-um upgrade is worth every penny near the water.
  • Frame design and color. A simple flat or mansard roofline costs less than a dome or a panoramic "picture window" build.
  • Permitting. A new enclosure has to meet Florida wind-load code and be permitted. We handle that, but it's part of the cost.

A real example from the field

Last storm season a homeowner in Pembroke Pines called us after their old screen room came apart. They assumed they needed a full rebuild — several of the quotes they'd gotten were for exactly that. When we walked it, the aluminum frame was still solid. All it needed was a full rescreen and a few new fasteners. They spent a fraction of what they'd braced for. That's the honest part of this business most people don't hear: half the time you don't need what you think you need.

My advice before you get quotes

Don't price a patio off a phone call alone. A good contractor comes out, measures, and looks at your actual structure. If someone quotes you a firm number sight-unseen, be careful — they're either padding it or they'll "find" extra costs later.

And get the scope in writing: mesh type, frame color, whether the permit is included, and the warranty. A $500 difference between two quotes usually comes down to one of those details.
