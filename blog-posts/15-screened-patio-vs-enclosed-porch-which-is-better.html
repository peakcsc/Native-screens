This is a real fork in the road, and the right answer depends entirely on how you want to use the space. I build screened patios, but I'll tell you honestly when an enclosed porch is the better call — because the worst outcome is spending the money on the wrong one.

What each one actually is

A screened patio is an open-air room: aluminum frame, screen mesh, breeze passing through, bugs and leaves kept out. An enclosed porch (glass or solid walls) is a sealed room — often with windows, sometimes with air conditioning — that keeps out weather entirely and can be climate-controlled.

Go with a screened patio if…

  • You want the outdoors — breeze, sounds, the pool — just without bugs.
  • You want it cooler and shadier than open sun, but still open-air.
  • You want the most usable space for the money.
  • You want it done faster and simpler.

For most South Florida families, this is the sweet spot. You live outside most of the year here; a screened patio lets you do that in comfort.

Go with an enclosed porch if…

  • You want a year-round, air-conditioned room you can use in August heat or a cold snap.
  • You need it fully sealed from rain, dust, and noise.
  • You want true privacy and security like an interior room.

The tradeoff is cost and complexity — glass enclosures run considerably more and are a bigger construction project.

The honest middle ground

A lot of homeowners get the best of both by doing a screened enclosure with a solid aluminum roof over part of it. You get shade and rain protection where you sit, open screen for airflow everywhere else, and you spend far less than glassing the whole thing in. I suggest this combination more than any other.

My take

If your goal is "enjoy the outdoors without bugs," a screened patio wins on value and lifestyle nearly every time down here. If your goal is "add a sealed, air-conditioned room," that's an enclosed porch — a different, bigger project. Be honest with yourself about which one you'll actually use, and build that.
