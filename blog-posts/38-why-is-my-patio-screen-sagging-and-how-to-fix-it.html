Baggy, wavy, saggy screen is one of the most common complaints I hear — and one of the most fixable. It's also a great early warning sign, because sagging usually means something specific has let go. Here's why it happens and what actually fixes it.

The usual culprits

  • Dried-out spline. The number one cause. The rubber cord that holds your screen tight shrinks and loses grip in our heat, so the screen loosens and waves. (This is often an easy fix.)
  • Stretched, aging mesh. Old screen loses its tension over the years and starts to droop, especially on big panels.
  • Heat expansion. Screen expands and contracts with temperature; cheaper or under-tensioned installs sag more as they cycle.
  • A poor original install. If the screen was never pulled tight or the spline was the wrong size, it was destined to sag. This is the most common issue I see on DIY and cut-rate jobs.
  • Impact or a frame that's racked out of square from a hit or settling.

How to fix it, from easiest to most involved

  • Re-seat the spline. If the spline is still flexible and just lifted, pressing it back into the channel with a spline roller can re-tension a panel. Quick and cheap.
  • Replace the spline. If it's brittle or cracked, new spline restores the grip. Often done together with the next step.
  • Rescreen the panel. If the mesh itself is old and stretched, new screen pulled tight is the real fix — re-splining tired mesh just delays it.
  • Address the frame if it's bent or out of square, so the new screen has something true to grip.

Can you DIY it?

Re-seating loose spline on one panel is a reasonable DIY if you're handy. But if you re-tension it and it sags again in a few weeks, that's your sign the mesh is done, not the spline — and chasing it is a waste. At that point a proper rescreen with the right tension and spline size solves it for years.

My honest take

Sagging is almost never a reason to panic or replace the whole enclosure. Usually it's spline, sometimes it's tired mesh. The fix is cheap and fast — and if a contractor tries to turn "my screen is a little loose" into a full rebuild, get a second opinion.
