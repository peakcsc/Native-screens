When homeowners plan a screened patio, they usually focus on the screen and forget the details that actually make the space livable for years. After building these since 1972, here are the features I tell people not to skip — and a couple of nice-to-haves that are worth it in South Florida.

The features I consider essential

  • The right mesh for your spot. Near water or canals, no-see-um mesh isn't a luxury — it's the difference between using the patio at dusk and getting eaten alive.
  • A solid screen door that latches. The door takes the most abuse of anything out there. Spend on a good one with quality rollers and a self-closing latch, or you'll be chasing bugs through a door that never shuts right.
  • Proper drainage and, often, super gutters. Our rain comes hard. Without good drainage or super gutters, water sheets off the enclosure and pools around your slab. This is the detail cheap builds skip.
  • Code-rated framing and anchoring. Not glamorous, but it's what keeps your enclosure attached to your house in a storm.

Features worth the upgrade

  • Pet-resistant mesh on the lower panels if you have dogs or cats. It saves you from re-screening the bottom every year.
  • Privacy screen on street- or neighbor-facing sides, so you can actually relax.
  • A kick plate at the base of doors and high-traffic panels to take foot and pet wear.
  • A panoramic "picture window" panel on a view side — if you've got a lake or golf course, it's worth removing the cross-bars to frame it.

Where people overspend

You don't need the fanciest roofline or the tallest cage to enjoy a patio. I've seen folks pour money into dramatic height and elaborate shapes, then live in the space exactly the same as they would in a simpler build. Put the budget into the mesh, the door, and the drainage — the things you touch and use every day.

A quick story

We did a lanai in a coastal neighborhood where the homeowner wanted to save by using standard screen throughout. I pushed them to at least do no-see-um on the pool-facing side. A month later they called — not with a complaint, but to say they finally sit out there at sunset for the first time in years. Those small, right choices are what make the space actually get used.
