South Florida has a way of turning a quiet afternoon into an emergency — a storm rolls through, a branch comes down, and suddenly your enclosure is torn open. Not every screen problem is urgent, but some are. Here's how I tell homeowners to sort the "handle it Monday" from the "call now."

What counts as a real emergency

  • Storm damage that compromised the structure — a bent beam, lifted anchors, or a section torn loose. That's not a screen problem anymore; it's a safety problem.
  • A large opening torn in the screen before more weather is coming. One torn panel spreads fast in the next windy day, and an open enclosure lets everything in.
  • A screen door blown off or jammed open so you can't secure the space.
  • Damage around a pool where an open cage becomes a safety and critter issue.

If the structure is affected or more weather is on the way, don't wait — get it looked at.

What can usually wait

  • A single small tear or hole with no storm coming — patch it and schedule a proper fix.
  • Faded, chalky screen that's simply old — that's a planned rescreen, not an emergency.
  • A sticky roller or loose spline — annoying, not urgent.

What to do right after a storm

  • Walk the enclosure and look for bent frame, torn panels, and lifted anchors — from a safe distance if anything looks unstable.
  • Photograph everything before any repairs, in case you file an insurance claim. Storm damage to an enclosure is often covered.
  • Don't ignore small tears — they spread in the next wind.
  • Call early. After a storm, every screen company in South Florida is slammed. The homeowners who get handled fastest are the ones who got on the list first.

My honest take

Here's the part people don't expect me to say: a torn screen is often a good sign. Screen panels are designed to blow out before the aluminum frame fails — that's by design, and a lost panel is far cheaper than a bent structure. So after a storm, a few torn panels usually means the enclosure did its job. Document it, call early, and we'll get you back together.
