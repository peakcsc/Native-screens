I've seen too many homeowners inherit a headache because a previous "handyman" skipped the permit. Let me explain how this really works in South Florida and when you do and don't need one.

When a permit is required

A screen enclosure is a permanent structure attached to your home, so a new patio enclosure, screen room, or pool cage requires a building permit in Broward, Miami-Dade, and Palm Beach. It has to be engineered and inspected to meet the Florida Building Code and our wind-load requirements — and in parts of Miami-Dade, the stricter High-Velocity Hurricane Zone standard.

That permit isn't red tape for its own sake. It's what makes sure your enclosure survives storm season instead of ending up in your neighbor's pool.

When you usually don't need one

If you're simply rescreening — replacing the mesh on an enclosure that's already standing — you typically don't need a permit, because you're not changing the structure. Same with swapping a torn screen door panel. We'll always tell you honestly which bucket your project falls into.

Who pulls the permit

This is the part that matters: a licensed contractor pulls the permit for you. That's one of the biggest reasons to hire licensed and insured rather than chasing the cheapest unlicensed price. The license is literally what allows the work to be permitted and inspected.

What happens if you skip it

I've walked into this situation more than once. An unpermitted enclosure can come back to bite you when you:

  • Sell your home — unpermitted structures show up in inspections and can kill or delay a sale.
  • File an insurance claim — after a storm, insurers can deny claims on unpermitted work.
  • Get flagged by the county — which can mean fines or being told to tear it out.

Fixing it after the fact is almost always more expensive and more painful than doing it right the first time.

My take

In 50-plus years I've never regretted pulling a permit, and I've never met a homeowner who regretted it either. The ones who regret things are the ones who went with the "don't worry about it" quote to save a few dollars. Do it right, build it to code, and you'll sleep through hurricane season.
